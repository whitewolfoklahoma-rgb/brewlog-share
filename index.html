ABV & attenuation

ABV = (OG − FG) × 131.25 · Apparent attenuation = (OG − FG) ÷ (OG − 1) × 100. The standard quick formulas — see Help for notes.

Priming sugar (bottle conditioning)

Accounts for the CO₂ already dissolved in the beer at its warmest post-fermentation temperature. An estimate — when in doubt, prime on the low side: you can drink a slightly soft beer, but over-carbonation risks gushers and bottle bombs. Typical targets: British ales 1.5–2.0, most American styles 2.2–2.7, wheat beers 2.7–3.2.

Strike water temperature

Palmer's infusion equation. An estimate — a cold mash tun steals a few extra degrees, so many brewers add 2–5°F or preheat the tun.

Dilution / top-up

Gravity points are conserved: new points = old points × (old volume ÷ new volume).

How BrewLog works

The idea

One file, no account, no subscription, no cloud — your brew records never leave this device. Log each batch as you brew it, save the recipes you'd brew again, and use the calculators on brew day. Manual entry is the feature: nothing syncs, nothing tracks you, and the file is yours forever.

The formulas (exactly what the calculators run)

ABV = (OG − FG) × 131.25. The standard quick formula; very strong beers read slightly low versus the advanced formula, which is normal for every homebrew calculator.

Apparent attenuation = (OG − FG) ÷ (OG − 1.000) × 100%. Most ale yeasts land at 65–85%.

Priming sugar. Beer already holds dissolved CO₂ from fermentation, estimated from its warmest post-fermentation temperature T (°F): residual vols = 3.0378 − 0.050062×T + 0.00026555×T². Sugar needed = (target vols − residual vols) × liters of beer × factor, where the factor is 4.41 g per liter per volume for corn sugar (dextrose) and 3.81 for table sugar (sucrose). This is an estimate: actual carbonation varies with how much CO₂ really stayed dissolved. Prime conservatively — over-carbonation can burst bottles.

Strike water (Palmer): strike temp = (0.2 ÷ ratio) × (target mash temp − grain temp) + target mash temp, with ratio in quarts of water per pound of grain. It assumes a preheated tun; add a few degrees for a cold one.

Dilution. Gravity points (SG − 1) × 1000 are conserved: new gravity = 1 + (old points × old volume ÷ new volume) ÷ 1000.

Batches & recipes

A recipe is a template. "Brew this" copies the recipe into a new batch — a snapshot. Editing the recipe later never rewrites the batch you already brewed, because the batch is the historical record. Ratings are your own 1–5 taste score; the dashboard average counts finished batches you've rated.

Your data

Everything is stored in this browser's local storage on this device only. Clearing your browser's site data erases it — export backups from Settings & Backup and keep the file somewhere safe. A backup from this app can be re-imported into any copy of BrewLog.

Honest limits & disclaimers

BrewLog is a hobby record-keeping and estimating tool. The calculators implement the standard published formulas and are estimates, not measurements — your hydrometer, thermometer, and scale are the authority. Homebrewing is legal for adults in many places but the rules vary by country, state, and locality, and some places restrict or prohibit it — know and follow your local laws. Drink responsibly, and never sell homebrew where that isn't licensed.
